Whispers of Motion

Among a billion still stars, a few things move: asteroids, comets, maybe a hidden world. Their motion is only a whisper between two images. Blink between two moments of NASA's SPHEREx infrared sky and watch one of them move.

How it works

Everything runs on public data with open Python tools (astropy, astroquery, reproject).

Find the images

Ask NASA/IPAC IRSA for every SPHEREx image covering a point in the sky. SPHEREx revisits each spot several times over hours, then again every six months.

Pick two moments

Choose two exposures from the same detector a few hours apart: long enough for an asteroid to move, short enough to keep it in frame.

Clean and align

Remove the zodiacal glow, mask bad pixels and cosmic rays, and re-project image B onto image A's pixel grid.

Subtract

Stars sit still and cancel out. Anything that moved leaves a red–blue pair: red where it was, blue where it went.

Check with JPL

Ask NASA JPL's Small-Body Identification service which known asteroids should be there, and compare predicted and measured motion.

What's next

Search many fields automatically, train a classifier to separate real movers from star residuals, and flag movers JPL doesn't know about.

Honest limits

SPHEREx sees each image at a slightly different infrared wavelength, so stars don't cancel perfectly and leave residuals. One exposure only reaches about magnitude 17–18, so fainter asteroids need many images stacked. Predicted positions use Earth's centre rather than SPHEREx's exact orbit, which explains the small, consistent offset of a few pixels. We do not claim to have found Planet X: it is the motivation, not the result.
